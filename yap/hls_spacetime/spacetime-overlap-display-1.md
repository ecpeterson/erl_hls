# Coordinate overlap and schematic routing

The scene previously deduplicated native primitive identities but drew tile coordinates. Distinct cells, PIPs and wires therefore produced coincident geometry; fanout arc identities could also produce identical chords. Square-prism roofs additionally shared a plane at same-clock crossings. The two color modes were behaving differently by design: Flow direction supplied endpoint gradients, whereas complete-path delay supplied a single cost through each segment. Uniform color along a complete critical path does not imply zero incremental delay.

The routing export binds native connectivity, PIP positions and unordered node wire-to-tile membership. It does not contain ordered metal polylines or track offsets. Prepared spans join source/PIP/sink tile positions by straight chords. Diagonals are schematic chords, not native diagonal metal. Replacing them with guessed Manhattan bends would overstate the evidence. The 3D view now calls this Routing connectivity and draws all such links dashed, including axis-aligned chords. Selected semantic dependency and static-path connectors are also dashed. Native tile/PIP markers and retained evidence remain unchanged.

`circuitDisplay` combines coincident point positions and exact/reversed spans, and sweeps partial axis-aligned overlaps into disjoint display spans. Membership, function roles, maximum eligible complete-path delay and unknown contributors are aggregated before coloring. Opposed directions stay neutral; compatible directed spans retain a source-to-sink gradient. Unresolved filtering happens before aggregation. These are display aggregates, not electrically connected nets. The original native identities and evidence tables are untouched. Partially overlapping diagonal chords are not split; exact duplicate diagonals are combined.

Storage overlays similarly union vertical intervals at each position. Access markers use selection priority; repeated dependency overlays draw once. Original event/storage hit targets remain separate. Diamond-section prisms avoid broad coplanar roofs at crossings; strict depth comparison resolves exact ties without inventing track or height offsets. Separate highlighted overlays and partial diagonal overlaps can still coincide, so this is not a claim to have eliminated every overlap or recovered physical tracks.
