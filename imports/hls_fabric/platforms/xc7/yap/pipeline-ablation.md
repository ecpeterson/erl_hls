Preregistered after private #41 at `91aec93`. Start from the completed candidate, retaining its independent admission, aggregate replacement fix, completion capacity and exact arithmetic. Remove each boundary separately: loader ownership transfer, mandatory completion retention, mandatory loaded-request retention, and committed-only selection. The last experiment permits current captures/retirement to inform selection but must continue excluding actors whose state write is not visible. It does not restore the obsolete lookahead register.

Measure two and four actors per scheduler. Actor count is fixed workload, not a knob for manufacturing utilization. For each family record eligible work, in-flight population, accepted requests/results, empty executor input and blocked transfers. Count steady-state intervals separately from startup/drain. A nonempty mailbox is not necessarily eligible work; reductions, ownership and egress reservations matter.

Hypotheses before implementation: removing loader or completion retention may recover roughly 16 cycles/step in the two-actor probe, as their insertion did. That is a scenario, not a bound or a prediction for four actors. Removing request retention should recover feedback time but may restore the previously measured 12.159 ns RAM-to-arithmetic family. Removing committed selection may recover capture visibility latency while rejoining arbitration/control cones. Expect interactions: route the one-boundary comparisons and a promising combination, rather than assuming marginal benefits add. Do not promise a frequency gain from primitive-depth changes.

Compare cycles × routed period, FF/LUT cost and affected endpoint families. Reuse unchanged reference routes and use the same mapping, 5 ns constraint and Vivado Default placement. One placement screens large changes; small differences are not statistically established. Follow the strongest candidate with the complete 2×4-qubit board workload (noise, transport and reduction traffic), keeping any missing coverage explicit. Do not introduce another pipeline stage until these comparisons justify the boundaries already present.

## Early checks

The first loader-removal attempt kept the separate loader's write-acknowledgement→read token edge inside the now-atomic metadata activation. XLS correctly required three stages. Restore the original integrated service's ordering instead: reads follow prior-write tokens, current writes follow read-request tokens, and acknowledgements still gate completion of the whole activation. This version schedules at the fixed two stages and passes both BEAM comparisons. It is the measured loader ablation.

The first metadata-forwarding attempt also forwarded newly captured credits. The diagnostic trace shows credits arriving in the same cycle as the configuration effect batches at 245/255/265/275 ns. Consuming such a credit before recording the newly published batch leaves the busy flag set with no future credit. It stops before step 8. Keeping credit capture registered, while forwarding the other captures/retirement into arbitration, passes both readiness patterns. This corrected experiment removes committed-only selection, not the independently necessary credit ordering rule. The failed experiment is retained as a negative witness, never as a timing sample.

All four corrected single-boundary removals measure 155.5 cycles/step on four actors versus 171.5 with all boundaries. The first three also measure 127.25 cycles/step on two actors versus 143.25. The observed four-actor completion ablation routes at 12.436 ns, beyond its 11.896 ns break-even period, so that boundary has a demonstrated benefit in this placement.

Next interaction screen, registered before building: remove both consecutive pre-executor boundaries (loader and loaded-request register), retaining committed selection and completion retention. If the individually observed latency savings add, expect 139.5 cycles/step for four actors and 111.25 for two. Re-exposing RAM→arithmetic is the main timing risk. Break-even against the completed candidate is 13.260 ns for four actors and 13.551 ns for two; these are acceptance thresholds, not forecasts. This tests whether one retained cut around a region is worth more than paying for two adjacent boundaries. Do not assume the single-removal results predict the paired route.

The paired experiment meets those cycle predictions but routes at 13.375 ns with four actors, missing break-even. Removing only the loader is the replay winner.
