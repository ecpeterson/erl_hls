## Subsequent control pipeline

[Rhee et al., *High-Level Synthesis of Efficient Pipelines with Visibility Control*, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18765v1#S3) organize hazards around publishing a pending access, finalizing its value, and committing it. Apply that distinction to our scheduler rather than introducing speculation: reservation publishes the actor identity; execution produces a result; commit updates authoritative storage. A later activation of that actor waits until commit unless an explicit, final-value bypass is provided. Independent actors may overlap.

Before each boundary change, tabulate the owner, read point, reservation, final-value point and commit/release point for actor state, mailbox metadata and output capacity. Keep round-robin grants tied to accepted reservations. Pending mailbox writes must be visible to arbitration; a stale ready snapshot is not an admissible shortcut. State reset must invalidate every pending transaction. Stage count and hazard policy remain separate choices. The paper does not establish correctness for our cross-proc implementation; tests and invariants must cover that boundary separately.
